The theory

Dead Internet Theory is the idea that a large and growing share of online activity is no longer driven by real people. Bots, automated scripts and AI-generated content have, according to the theory, quietly taken over much of what we experience as the internet — filling feeds, inflating engagement counts and crowding out genuine human interaction.

The theory circulates mostly as speculation, but it points at something worth measuring: how much of a given platform is actually automated, and can that be detected from the outside?

This project picks Bluesky as its case study. Bluesky is a newer social platform with a fully open public API, which makes it possible to collect the data needed for a systematic study without needing special access.

Many Bluesky accounts openly label themselves as bots — news feeds, weather alerts, sports scores. That honesty makes them useful: they form a ground-truth group of real, confirmed automation that can be compared against ordinary accounts.

Why this topic

The question of how much online activity is genuinely human has become harder to ignore as AI-generated content grows. I wanted to approach it empirically rather than speculatively — to build a tool that makes actual measurements instead of relying on intuition or anecdote.

Dead Internet Theory is easy to dismiss as a conspiracy theory, but the underlying concern — that bots distort what we see and how we interact online — is worth taking seriously. This project is an attempt to find out how well that concern can be quantified.

What the bot checker measures

The Bot Checker reads a Bluesky account's public data — its profile, up to 100 recent posts, and up to 100 followers — and turns that into 37 behavioural signals. Each signal describes one measurable aspect of how the account behaves: how often it posts, how regular its timing is, how similar its posts are to each other, and what kind of accounts follow it.

Those signals are grouped into four categories — Profile, Timing, Content, and Social graph — and compared against the study's bot and ordinary groups using a logistic regression model. The result is a score from 0 to 100 and a band label describing how much the account resembles the declared bots.

Everything runs in your browser. No data is stored or sent anywhere except to Bluesky's public API.
